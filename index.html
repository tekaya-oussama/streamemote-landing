Signals

Three vitals, zero hardware.

Your camera already sees more than you think. Skin brightens and dims with every heartbeat — too faintly for you to notice, plenty for a 30 fps sensor.

Heart rate Every 1s

Remote photoplethysmography tracks blood volume in your face frame by frame. Same principle as the pulse oximeter on your fingertip — just done with light your webcam already collects.

Accuracy±3 bpm
Band0.7–4.0 Hz
RegionForehead + cheeks
Breathing rate Every 2s

Chest rise and the micro-movements around your nose and shoulders, measured against a stable background. No strap, no mic, no calibration breath to hold.

Range6–40 br/min
Window20 s rolling
NeedsHead in frame
Stress Every 5s

Derived from heart rate variability — the gap between beats, which shortens and steadies when you're under load. Reported as Low, Moderate or High, with the raw RMSSD if you want it.

Privacy

Your face never leaves the room.

A plugin that reads your pulse off your face should have to explain itself. So: every frame is analysed on your own CPU, in the same process as OBS, and then discarded. There is no upload step to opt out of, because there is no upload.

No video, ever Frames are read from memory and dropped. Nothing is written to disk or sent anywhere.
Only numbers reach the overlay Three integers and a confidence flag. That's the entire payload crossing into your scene.
Works with the network off Pull your ethernet cable mid-stream and the vitals keep reading. Try it — it's the cleanest proof we can offer.
Readable source for the capture path The camera and signal-processing modules are public. Audit them before you trust them.

Setup

Four steps, one stream to spare.

No calibration routine, no warm-up window, no baseline session before you can go live.

Step 01

Install the plugin

One installer for Windows, macOS and Linux. It registers itself as an OBS source — no manual folder surgery.

Step 02

Point your webcam

Any camera OBS can already see. StreamEmote reads the same feed your viewers do, so there's no second device to frame.

Step 03

Pick an overlay

Minimal badge, full dashboard, or a single number. Drag to position, set your colours, save it to the scene.

Step 04

Go live

Vitals appear within about ten seconds of your face entering frame, and stay there.

On stream

More than a number in the corner.

Once your vitals are a live signal, the rest of your scene can listen to them.

Clutch alerts Cross a heart-rate threshold and fire a screen shake, a sound, or a lower-third banner. The moment announces itself.
Auto-highlight clips Every heart-rate spike gets timestamped and clipped. Your VOD editor opens to a list instead of six hours of footage.
Heart-rate donations Viewers tip to set a challenge — hit 150 bpm, or hold Low stress for five minutes — and watch your vitals answer.
Merged co-stream vitals Run a duo or a squad and put everyone's heart rate in one panel. The spike that matters is usually someone else's.
Honest by default

When the signal is bad, we say so.

Dim lighting, a turned head, a lean out of frame — rPPG degrades, and a plugin that kept printing a confident 72 would be lying to your chat. StreamEmote shows its confidence instead, and drops the number rather than invent one.
